Welcome! I want to share what I've learned about MARC and RDA with you. The best way to learn is to teach afterall!

MARC (MAchine Readable Catalog) records are used to catalog items in library collections to make them assessable to both the library works and the library’s patrons. This is what allows you to look up keywords to find a book you want on the MU libraries website. RDA (Resource Description and Access) was an update to how these items are described and how people can search for them. The Library of Congress maintains and decides these standards. They have guides to understanding them. These records hold everything about a cataloged item: the edition, volume, height, material specifications, publication information, and more (Library of Congress). These computerized records are important because they make libraries more accessible, which fulfils one of the most important tenants of library science: Books are for use. Libraries exist to provide information. At times that information is research material for students, or bedtime stories for children, all of it is important, and all of it falls into these MARC records.

The Five Laws of Library Science, are:
Books are for use.
Every person has his or her book.
Every book has its reader.
Save the time of the reader.
A library is a growing organism.
